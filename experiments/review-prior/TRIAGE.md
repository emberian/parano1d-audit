## Additional deployment-facing observations

1. **W7 receive-address substitution:** a process answering the local RPC port can supply an
   unrelated address as the GUI backend's active receive address; an independent
   pinned wallet builder signed a synthetic payment to it
   (`wallet-boundary/README.md`). The Copy Address UI and a mined recipient
   outcome were source-traced, not executed. This is the clearest conditional
   destination-substitution path: no wallet key is needed, but local RPC or
   software-distribution control is.
2. **Wallet send acknowledgment:** an isolated sender returned success and a
   txid while an independent receiver had no mempool entry; after connection
   the receiver learned the same txid (`operator-exit/REPORT.md`). This can
   cause a user to read local admission as deposit delivery. Exchange credit
   and peer delivery are separate states.
3. **Finality off by one:** source permits replacing an 18-block suffix while
   docs imply a 17-block maximum (`chain-malice/REPORT.md`). At 18
   tip-inclusive confirmations a recipient may still be reorged; 19 cross the
   local boundary. Double-spend significance depends on an exchange or merchant
   actually crediting at 18 and production of a heavier valid fork.

For a comparison of holder deposit and exchange sale paths, the measured
behaviors are local wallet endpoint substitution, acknowledgment before peer
delivery, and ordinary producer discretion. A combined deposit obstruction
and independent sale path has not been measured. Native NOID, native assets,
and exchange balances remain distinct parts of that study.
