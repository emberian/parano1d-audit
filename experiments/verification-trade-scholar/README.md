# Trade selection: source review of NM-3A/B

Target: pinned Parano1d source at `52938433a30b185ab007afaa15166a632cf2e82e`. This note is source review only. It does not claim an admitted malicious trade, a produced block, or network-wide behavior.

## In ordinary terms

The block format gives a miner 64 **funding-input places** for all trades together, across up to four markets. Think of a delivery van with 64 parcel places: a trade that brings 64 separate funding parcels could fill the van even though 47 trade-policy places remain. Other trades wait for another van. That is the **budget** claim (NM-3A). The prospective harm is one block of delayed trading across markets, or repeated delays if the costly conditions recur. It is not a halted chain, a rejected ordinary NOID transfer, or a consensus rule forbidding another producer from choosing different transactions.

The **assembly** claim (NM-3B) concerns selection *within one market*. The miner tentatively builds a trade batch, then recomputes its execution whenever it considers another trade. Execution favors buys or sells according to the current price, not simply their earlier priority ranking. A later-ranked trade could therefore execute first, change the price, make previously selected trades miss their minimum-return or fee ceiling, and leave the miner with a smaller batch. The affected traders' policies are skipped for that candidate block; the source does not show their funds stolen or their transactions invalidated.

## What the pinned code supports

- The v2.1 Native budget derives shared `markets`, `policies`, and `funding` counters from the proof-bank configuration; `try_consume` subtracts a group's funding use from that shared counter. See [miner budget](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_miner/src/template.rs#L652) and [budget subtraction](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_chain/src/mempool.rs#L472). `trade_policy_resources` counts one funding unit per input; admission checks whether **one policy** fits a class budget. See [resource calculation](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_chain/src/mempool.rs#L356) and [mempool check](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_mempool/src/trade.rs#L91). The pinned bank's 64-input Native value is decoded in Gemini's [metadata table](../comp-system-hunter/native-market-seam/NOTES.md#pinned-v21-proof-bank-parameters-decoded-from-v21-runtime-metadatabin); we did not independently execute the decoder in this source-only review.
- In template assembly, assets appear in order of their first ranked policy. A market group that uses all 64 funding places leaves no funding for later market groups; within the same group, an added policy above the remaining budget is skipped. See [ranking/grouping](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_miner/src/template/trades.rs#L424), [funding check](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_miner/src/template/trades.rs#L465), and [committing a group](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_miner/src/template/trades.rs#L507). This is conditional on the large trade having a fill, fitting the full assembled group's other limits, and being selected ahead of the competitors.
- A 64-input policy is structurally within the general 1,020-input cap and a single-owner V4 authorization can bind its complete intent hash. But admission checks the real funding slots against State and verifies the V4 proof; a forged or merely synthetic policy will not pass. See [funding validation](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_tx/src/native_assets/trade.rs#L137), [State check](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_mempool/src/trade.rs#L132), [proof verification](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_mempool/src/trade.rs#L27), and [proof construction](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_gkr/src/wallet_authorization.rs#L542). The standard RPC planner stops gathering cash inputs once it has enough; it does not offer a simple “use 64 inputs” control. See [trade funding loop](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_rpc/src/server/native/planner.rs#L872). None of this establishes that a 64-input policy was actually generated, admitted, and included.
- **Priority correction:** A large input amount determines *which* input serves as an anchor; order is a digest of parent block ID, asset, anchor creation ID and slot. Amount is not itself the priority score. Thus Gemini's “one principal input to control priority” overstates what the source shows. See [priority calculation](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_tx/src/native_assets/trade.rs#L230). Another market can be considered first, and the miner compares class candidates by claimable fees; the Native candidate does not automatically win. See [class comparison](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_mempool/src/mining.rs#L166).
- `stable_policies` reruns sequencing for the entire proposed set, drops unfilled or unchargeable policies, and repeats. `assemble` can then assign this smaller set over the previously accepted set without checking that it kept the old members or their fees. See [batch recheck](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_miner/src/template/trades.rs#L261) and [replacement](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_miner/src/template/trades.rs#L465). Sequencing starts from ranked priorities but chooses the currently preferred buy/sell side at each step. See [side preference](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_tx/src/native_assets/trade/sequencing.rs#L113). The source therefore supports a **conditional nonmonotonic selection path**. It does not by itself prove Gemini's exact graduation and slippage example can be funded and assembled on a real market state.
- **Batch-size correction:** Gemini says up to 47 earlier sells might be displaced. A sell needs at least one token-balance input and one positive-NOID input to cover its fee: token balances carry zero NOID in the raw amount lane. See [sell funding](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_tx/src/native_assets/trade.rs#L155), [fee funding](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_tx/src/native_assets/trade.rs#L182), and [token carrier amount](../comp-system-hunter/pinned/noid_tx/src/native_assets.rs#L618). Under the shared 64-funding-input limit, at most **31 two-input sells plus a one-input buy** can enter the proposed trial; other resource limits may lower this further. The number 47 counts policy slots while ignoring funding slots.

## Evidentiary boundary

NM-3A is a resource-accounting design concern with a conditional miner-selection consequence. NM-3B is a conditional selection-regression path in the source. Neither currently has a valid admitted-and-mined example, an observed mainnet trader delay, a demonstrated profitable repetition strategy, or evidence of operator-specific power. Trade inclusion also depends on the active Native class winning the miner's revenue comparison; ordinary block production can proceed without these trades. The two claims should remain leads, with Gemini's numerical and priority claims corrected as above.
